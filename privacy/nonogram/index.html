Privacy Policy

Nonogram — Picture Cross · Last updated 7 September 2026

Short version: the app has no accounts and does not identify you. Everything you play stays on your device. The app also sends anonymous counts of how the game is used — never your name, your location, or anything that points back to you — and you can switch that off in Settings.

1. Who we are

Nonogram — Picture Cross is published by GadGames. Contact: support@tug.today.

2. Information we collect

Nothing that identifies you. We do not ask for your name, email, phone number or location, there is no way to create an account, and no advertising identifier is requested. What we do receive is described in section 5.

The app stores the following only on your device, using the operating system's normal app storage: the puzzle you are in the middle of, completion statistics and streaks, which packs and pictures you have finished, your coin balance, and your settings. We cannot see any of it.

The app never asks you for a permission. It does not use the camera, the photo library, the microphone, contacts or location, and no permission prompt is ever shown. It uses the network for one thing only: sending the anonymous events in section 5.

3. Purchases

There are two kinds. Coins are a consumable purchase, bought in packs and spent on hints. Unlimited Hints is a one-time non-consumable unlock. You can also earn a coin without paying, by solving the daily challenge. No part of any puzzle is locked behind a purchase, and there is no subscription.

Purchases are handled entirely by the App Store. We never see or receive your payment details. The store tells the app whether a purchase completed, so that it can credit what you paid for, and reports sales to us only as aggregate figures that identify nobody.

Restoring a purchase asks the store whether your account owns the Unlimited Hints unlock. That exchange is between your device and the store; no account with us is created or needed. Coins, being consumable, cannot be restored by any app — that is how the store works rather than a choice we made.

4. Advertising

The app contains no advertising. There is no advertising SDK in it, no advertising identifier is requested, and nothing about you is shared with any advertising network or data broker.

If that ever changes — the only format we would ever add is a short video you choose to watch in exchange for a coin — this page will be updated before the version that changes it is released, and the App Store privacy label will be updated with it.

5. Anonymous usage data

To learn which grid sizes are graded wrong, which pictures people put down, and whether the hints are teaching anything, the app sends a small set of anonymous events: the app being opened; a puzzle being started, solved or abandoned, along with its grid size, its difficulty, how long it took, how many hints were used and — when a puzzle is abandoned — how much of the grid was filled; the daily challenge being solved; a hint being taken; the store being opened; and a purchase or restore completing.

These events do not identify you. They carry a random string created on your device the first time the app runs. It is not your Apple ID or Google account, not your advertising identifier, and not derived from your hardware — it exists only so that sixty people solving one puzzle each can be told apart from one person solving sixty. Deleting the app destroys it, and reinstalling produces a new one that cannot be connected to the old.

The events are processed on our behalf by PostHog, which stores them without building any profile of a person. Your device's network address is visible to them in the moment of the request, as it is to any web server; it is not used to derive your location, and no location is stored.

You can turn this off. Settings → "Share anonymous usage data". Nothing is sent while it is off, and anything still queued on the device is discarded.

There is no crash-reporting service and no other analytics provider.

6. Children

The app is suitable for all ages. It does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, of any age, and the anonymous usage data in section 5 is not personal information and cannot be traced to a child or to anyone else.

7. Keeping and deleting data

Everything you play — progress, statistics, streaks, settings and coins — is held only on your device, so deleting the app deletes it. There is no account to close. The anonymous usage events in section 5 are kept for as long as they are useful for improving the game; because nothing in them identifies you, they cannot be traced back to a particular person or removed on request. If you would rather send none at all, switch the setting off.

8. Your rights

Laws such as the GDPR and the CCPA give you rights to access, correct, export and delete personal data held about you. We hold no personal data about you: no account, no contact details, and no identifier that points to you. There is therefore nothing for us to produce or erase. If you would like this confirmed in writing, email us and we will reply.

9. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it, and any change that affects what the app does will be published before the version that makes the change is released.
